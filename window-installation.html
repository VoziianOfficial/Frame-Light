What this category covers

Window installation starts with the right project scope.

Window installation can apply to new construction, additions, remodels, converted spaces, or projects where a homeowner wants to add windows where they did not previously exist. It may involve measurements, product selection, wall conditions, weatherproofing, trim details, and coordination with other project steps.

Because installation work depends heavily on the home, the opening, the product, and the provider’s process, homeowners should compare more than a single price. Written scope, warranty terms, product specifications, and timeline expectations are all important.

Frame & Light helps organize the quote request stage so homeowners can connect with independent providers and review their options directly.

Provider evaluation

Questions to ask before choosing an installer.

Frame & Light can help homeowners connect with providers, but the final review should happen directly between the homeowner and each company.

01

Required credentials

Ask whether the provider holds any license, insurance, bonding, or registration required for window installation in your area.

02

Measurement process

Confirm how openings are measured, who verifies measurements, and what happens if framing or wall conditions require adjustment.

03

Product specifications

Compare frame material, glass package, insulation, hardware, grid style, color, trim approach, and energy-performance details.

04

Written estimate

Review labor, materials, exclusions, payment terms, cleanup, disposal, permits, warranty coverage, and timeline details in writing.

Project factors

What may affect window installation provider choice.

Installation projects are not identical. Provider recommendations, pricing, and timeline can vary based on property conditions and project details.

01

Opening type and wall conditions

A new opening, structural change, or remodel condition may require a different provider process than installing a window into an existing prepared opening.

02

Window quantity and size

Large windows, multiple openings, specialty sizes, or upper-floor access can affect labor, equipment, and scheduling.

03

Frame and glass selections

Vinyl, fiberglass, wood, aluminum-clad, double-pane, triple-pane, low-E glass, and other options may influence provider recommendations.

04

Permits, cleanup, and warranty terms

Ask whether permits are needed, who handles disposal, how cleanup works, and what warranty coverage applies to product and labor.
